The Captain cannot work out why anyone would AI-generate a photo of their own food, and Mr. Green has the answer: they do not know how to hire people, and they would not pay them if they did. Descript quietly rebrands the same transcription it has run for six years as AI, Mad Men turns out to be a show about a man faking it rather than a marketing seminar, and the Empire gets a performance review on healthcare, railings, and what you call slavery when you have a brand to protect.

Then the rules get examined. Congress writes its own rules and blames them, The Wire explains what seniority did to the docks, and the Captain asks who has read less of their text: people with political opinions and the Constitution, or people with religious opinions and the Bible. Laws priced at "two years or $20,000" are not a choice, they are a tax bracket. AI is just a four-digit lock being tried in every order, every image and every alien already lives inside your TV, and so does every alien that does not exist, which makes it all nothing. Nobody wants politics in their gas prices until the gas prices show up.

Finally, the Moonshine gets weapons. Laser headlights that fire like machine guns, a red hood beam with a "do not press" sticker that shoots through space forever, a rear gun with light blue shots so the audience knows which gun is which, and a Skull Pirate fight laid out on a tic-tac-toe board. Road Rash II, Star Fox, and O Brother, Where Art Thou? all make the design meeting. The Captain does not believe in violence, but he will absolutely jump onto a pirate ship. Dolly Parton and George Carlin ride the dashboard.
