The Captain wants to know if the Patreon makes sense to anyone but him, and Mr. Green wants the numbers gone. Nobody wants to look like they are not worthy of the money, so the members, the monthly total, and the post count all get hidden, the Junior Rangers get a business pitch of a name, and the new members get hazed digitally once they are inside. Mr. Green also gets email and Instagram access, which means less transparency for you and more transparency for him, exactly as the partnership dictates.

Then the economy turns out to be the Captain's abusive boyfriend. Everyone else says give him another chance, and nobody believes the one guy getting hit in the margins while driving DoorDash. Mr. Green explains how Wall Street's love of tech margins ended up in your pocket at all hours, the Captain points out that capitalism needs competition and the billionaires are all circling the same cracker, and somewhere a Batman and a Joker are missing. Level zero is a bed, a bathroom, and a meal, a king who never leaves the palace keeps his throne, and a Lego letter from the fifth grade asks for fifteen percent.

Finally, the crew gets out of Draconaria before the story goes any further and takes a simple box pickup. The Captain plays the guard, Mr. Green stays in the car with his feet, and the only D1CK robot working today wants papers, a pressure plate, and a very long processing time. Nobody is sure which of them is the one doing the lashing, the show reminds itself it is cheap television, and the Moonshine pays 37 credits to find out that nothing was sped up.
